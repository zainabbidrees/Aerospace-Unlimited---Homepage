Accreditations

What we are certified to, and what each one covers.

All three are maintained by annual surveillance and periodic recertification audits carried out by independent bodies. Current certificates are issued on request.

AS9120B

Aviation, space and defence distributors

The distributor-specific aerospace quality standard. It governs how we control purchasing from approved sources, verify and retain traceability records, split and repackage lots without breaking the paper trail, and handle nonconforming or suspect material.

Evidence: certificate stating registration number, scope and expiry, plus the registrar's audit cycle.

ISO 9001:2015

Quality management system

The underlying management system AS9120B is built on: documented procedures, defined responsibilities, corrective and preventive action, internal audit, management review and measurable improvement targets on on-time delivery and quality escapes.

Evidence: certificate, quality manual index, and corrective-action procedure on request under NDA.

FAA AC 00-56B

Voluntary distributor accreditation

Accreditation against the FAA's voluntary programme for civil aircraft parts distributors, audited to an ASA-100 equivalent quality system. It is the accreditation most airline and MRO receiving departments look for before they will accept parts from a non-OEM source.

Evidence: accreditation certificate and listing details from the accrediting organisation.

Registration numbers, scope statements and expiry dates are on the certificates themselves.

Traceability & documentation

What arrives with the part.

Documentation is quoted, not negotiated afterwards. Every quote states which of these travels with the item, and nothing ships without the package your receiving inspector will ask for.

Documentation supplied with shipments
FAA Form 8130-3 Authorized Release Certificate, supplied where the item is certificated and the release is available. Dual release (8130-3 with EASA Form 1) is quoted on request for operators on both registers.
Certificate of Conformance Issued against the purchase order line, referencing part number, quantity, condition, lot or batch, and serial numbers where serialised.
Trace to source Documented chain of custody back to the originating OEM, airline, MRO, government contract or authorised distributor. We state the last certified source on the quote, before you commit.
Manufacturer certification OEM certificate, material certification or mill test report for raw and standard hardware, where the item type carries one.
Teardown documentation For salvaged material: teardown report, removal tag, and a non-incident / non-accident statement covering the donor airframe.
Shelf life and cure date Manufacture, cure and expiry dates recorded and visible on the packaging for age-controlled items. Remaining-life minimums can be specified on the RFQ.
Records retention Traceability records are retained and retrievable against the order for the period required by AS9120B, so a document can be re-issued years after delivery.

Counterfeit-parts avoidance

How suspect material is kept out of the supply chain.

Our programme follows the AS6081 and AS9120B requirements for preventing, detecting and reporting counterfeit and suspect unapproved parts. The written policy is available in the document pack.

Controlled sources first

Purchasing preference runs OEM, then OEM-authorised distributor, then approved surplus with documented trace. Every supplier is on an approved vendor list, qualified on their own quality accreditation and trace practice, and re-evaluated on a defined cycle with performance history attached.

Detection at receipt

Incoming material is examined for the known indicators: packaging and label inconsistency, re-marked or resurfaced parts, date and lot codes that disagree with the paperwork, altered documentation, and country-of-origin markings that do not match the declared source.

Quarantine, never return

Suspect material is impounded in quarantine and is not returned to the supplier and not resold — returning it simply puts it back into circulation. Disposition is controlled, recorded, and reported to the affected customer and to the appropriate authority and industry reporting programmes.

Flow-down to our suppliers

Counterfeit-avoidance, trace and records-retention requirements are written into our purchase orders, along with any FAR and DFARS clauses your contract flows down to us. Suppliers accept them as a condition of the order.

Receiving and inspection

What happens between our supplier and your dock.

Documents before the box

Paperwork is verified against the purchase order first — release certificate, C of C, trace to source, and lot or serial numbers. A shipment whose documents do not reconcile is held before anyone opens it.

Identity and quantity

Part number, revision or dash number, condition code, quantity and unit of issue are confirmed against both the PO and the physical marking on the item.

Physical inspection

Visual inspection for damage, corrosion, prior installation, re-marking and packaging integrity, with counterfeit indicators checked as part of the same pass.

Age and handling control

ESD-sensitive items are handled and packed under ESD control. Shelf-life items are recorded with cure and expiry dates and stocked so the oldest usable stock leaves first.

Stock, or quarantine

Conforming material is stocked with its records linked to the lot. Anything nonconforming or suspect is segregated in quarantine under the nonconforming material procedure — it does not sit on the shelf while a decision is made.

Export compliance

ITAR and EAR handled as part of the order.

All orders are fulfilled from the United States. Export classification and screening happen while the quote is being prepared, not after you have raised the purchase order.

ITAR-controlled articles

Defence articles subject to the International Traffic in Arms Regulations are handled under our registration and released only to eligible parties, with any required authorisation in place before shipment. Technical data is controlled to the same standard as the hardware.

EAR classification

Commercial and dual-use items are classified against the Commerce Control List. The applicable ECCN, or EAR99 status, is stated on the quote so your own trade team can review it before award.

Screening and end use

Parties to the transaction are screened against the restricted and denied party lists. End-use and end-user statements are collected where the item, the destination or the application requires them.

Origin and fulfilment

Country of origin is declared on the quote and on the shipping documents. Orders are picked, inspected and shipped from our facility in Anaheim, California, under full export-control compliance.
