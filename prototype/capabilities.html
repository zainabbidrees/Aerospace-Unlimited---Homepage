Service line 01

Ground support equipment & tooling

The equipment that never appears on an illustrated parts catalog, and the aircraft-specific special tooling a maintenance task calls out by name.

What this covers

Towbars and tow heads, axle and tripod jacks, ground power and air start units, hydraulic and pneumatic test stands, engine stands, slings and lifting gear, wheel and brake handling equipment, servicing carts, and the aircraft-specific special tooling named in AMM, CMM and SRM procedures. New, refurbished and approved-equivalent options are quoted side by side where they exist.

Who asks for it

Line and base maintenance teams hitting a task that needs a tool they do not hold. MROs adding a new type rating and building the tool list from scratch. Defence units and FBOs re-equipping a hangar. Anyone who has been quoted a nine-month lead time by the airframer and needs an alternative.

What to send us

  • Tool part number and the manual reference — the AMM, CMM or SRM chapter and task is often enough on its own if you do not have the number.
  • Aircraft type and effectivity — model, series and MSN range, since tooling frequently changes between blocks.
  • Whether an approved equivalent is acceptable — it is usually the difference between weeks and months.
  • Calibration or certification you need — proof-load test certificates, calibration due dates, and to whose standard.
  • Quantity, need-by date and ship-to — oversized and hazmat items are freight-quoted with the tool, not after.

Service line 02

PMA parts & supplements

Approved alternatives to the OEM part, for when the OEM line is long, the price is unworkable, or the part number has been discontinued outright.

What this covers

Parts produced under an FAA Parts Manufacturer Approval — a replacement or modification article approved for installation on a type-certificated product. We source across the PMA supplier base for structural hardware, consumables, interiors, seals and bearings, electrical components and rotable alternatives, and we identify the OEM number each one supersedes.

Who asks for it

Operators and MROs holding an AOG against an OEM lead time measured in months. Fleet engineering teams with PMA already accepted on their approved parts list and a cost target to hit. Support programmes for aircraft where the original supplier has exited the part number entirely.

Eligibility is your decision, and we give you what you need to make it.

PMA acceptance rests with the operator, and lease agreements or maintenance contracts can restrict it. Our quote states the approval basis, the OEM part number superseded, and the eligibility applicable to your type — before you commit, not after the box arrives.

What to send us

  • The OEM part number you are replacing — plus any alternate or superseded numbers you already know about.
  • Aircraft or engine type and effectivity — eligibility is granted against specific models, not against a part in the abstract.
  • Whether PMA is already accepted on your AVL — and whether a lease or maintenance agreement restricts it.
  • Quantity, condition and documentation required — new, overhauled or serviceable, and whether you need 8130-3.
  • Need-by date — tell us if you are grounded; it changes which suppliers we go to first.

Service line 03

Repair & overhaul

Send us a removed unit and we route it through our repair-station network for evaluation, repair or full overhaul — and return it with the release paperwork.

What this covers

Component repair and overhaul through certificated repair stations: avionics and instruments, hydraulics and actuation, pneumatics and valves, electrical and power generation, wheels and brakes, fuel components and accessories. Where a repair is not the fastest route, we quote exchange or outright replacement against the same request so you can choose on cost and turn time.

Who asks for it

Operators and MROs with a unit off the aircraft and no serviceable spare on the shelf, or with a component outside their own capability rating. Owners of legacy equipment where a repair is simply cheaper and faster than chasing a new unit that may no longer be produced.

Send the details, get shipping instructions

Raise the request with part and serial number and the fault. We return the routing, an RMA reference and the address to ship to — usually the same day.

Evaluation and a firm quote

The shop evaluates the unit and issues a findings report with a fixed price and turn time. If it is beyond economic repair you are told then, with the replacement or exchange price alongside, and nothing further is spent.

Repair, then return with paperwork

On your approval the work proceeds and the unit comes back with its release certificate — 8130-3, or dual release where your register requires it — and the shop report attached to the order record.

What to send us

  • Part number and serial number — plus the modification or software standard if the unit carries one.
  • Aircraft type and position — and the reason for removal, described as your technician described it.
  • Fault description and any fault codes — a vague "u/s" tag adds days at evaluation.
  • The outcome you want — repair, full overhaul, exchange, or outright replacement, if you already know.
  • Turn-time requirement — routine, expedited or AOG, and any last shop-visit paperwork you hold.

Service line 04

Salvaged aircraft parts

Teardown inventory with documented provenance — often the only remaining source for airframes and systems that are out of production.

What this covers

Material recovered from retired and parted-out aircraft: structure and skin panels, interiors and seating, avionics and instruments, actuators and valves, wiring harnesses, landing gear components and engine accessories. Quoted with a condition code — new surplus, overhauled, serviceable or as-removed — never as a single vague "used".

Who asks for it

Operators of legacy and out-of-production types where the OEM part number is closed. Military and government support programmes with platforms that have outlived their supply chain. Restoration and heritage programmes. Anyone facing a lead time longer than the aircraft can stay on the ground.

The paperwork that comes with it

Every salvaged line is offered with a teardown report and removal tag, trace back to the donor airframe, and a non-incident and non-accident statement covering that aircraft. Where the part is life-limited we supply the back-to-birth records or state plainly that they are not available — a buyer discovering that at receiving inspection is worse than not quoting at all. See our documentation policy →

What to send us

  • Part number, plus alternates and superseded numbers — legacy numbering changed hands often, and the alternates widen the search.
  • Aircraft type, series and effectivity — including MSN range where the part is block-specific.
  • Condition codes you can accept — telling us as-removed is acceptable can turn a six-week hunt into a same-week answer.
  • Documentation you must have — serviceable tag, 8130-3, back-to-birth trace, or teardown report only.
  • Quantity and need-by date — and whether you would take a partial shipment to get flying sooner.
